The feedback, in three lines

Diagnosis

A planar BSP in R³ makes the trivariate irrelevant, and a planar partition always comes apart. Dovetails can go anywhere, so there is no principled place for them.

Reframing

Cut in the parameter domain D instead. A plane in D is mapped by M to a curved surface in R³, and a curved interface can refuse a pull.

Test

Build a translational DBG with exact normals n′ ∝ J⁻ᵀn. Check for a single key, then a level k. Keep det J of M∘R positive.

First experiment: Elber's 2×2×2 puzzle with the pins removed, vary R, and tabulate blocked pairs, immobile pieces and the single key.

This is what the professor said last time, in my words. The current app cut with planes in R³ using a bounding-box trivariate, so the map was affine and the trivariate did nothing. A planar partition always comes apart because every pair can slide along its separating plane's normal. The proposal: cut in D, let M bend the cut, and test if the bend alone blocks. The harmonic field is for building the trivariate (Martin, Cohen and Kirby 2009), not for cutting.
